Science is about descriptive statements not prescriptive, which might lead one to think that science has nothing to say about how to live a good life. However, science provides us with a framework to understand reality, which helps us make informed choices, and cultivate a rational approach to living.

# Emotions and Reality
For mundane, let your emotions run wild - go crazy, have fun. But for anything significant, do not relay on emotions!!!

Emotions are highly variable and influenced by countless factors, such as sleep quality, diet, social interactions, and work environment. They are not a reliable guide to judge your abilities, potential, or interests.

These emotions are a part of being human. They can be seen as a time-saving trick, allowing us to make decisions without expending significant mental effort. However, for critical decisions, we should not rely on emotions. Instead, we should acknowledge their existence and let them pass without dwelling on them.

This concept is akin to "system 1 - system 2 thinking," as proposed by Daniel Kahneman, who won the Nobel prize in Economics for his monumental work on behavioural economics which provides a scientific approach to the IRRATIONAL decision making by humans. System 1 is fast, automatic, and emotional, while System 2 is slow, deliberate, and rational. For crucial decisions, we need to engage System 2.

Given that, for anything critical, we should not rely on emotions instead we should just accept their existence as a part of being irrational human beings. Trying to suppress them also doesn't work instead we should just let them flow, that is meditate on task at hand, on what you want to do; and whenever these negative thoughts occur do not hold on to them let them come and go, as there is no rational point to hold on to them. That's meditation.

To give you a personal example, I remember during my 4th year in PhD, I was studying something and encountered a specific integral and I had a tear in my eye, as I realised that I have read about this integral for at least 6 or 7 times by then, in past many years. I felt like I should have rememberd how to do it, I should have remember the answer. But fact of the matter is, it's irrelevant! It was simply irrelevant what should have been the case, what mattered was at that time I needed the solution of that integral to proceed, so the only logical/rational choice was to read it again and move on.

To learn anything and everything, You need to ensure 3 things -
1) absolute confidence in your ability to learn
2) your mistakes, limitations, scope of improvement should be VERY clearly in front of you
3) absence of any self-doubt, negative attitude etc

Interestingly, given that even without proper guidance, you'll figure out a way.

Given you ensure the above, to learn anything you need 2 separate types of activities -
- Craft Focused - learning the basics, basics chords on guitar, basic exercises in maths, again and again and again, regardless whether you can already do it or not. Just consider it as Meditation, relaxing while doing some repetitive simple tasks.
- Creativity Focused - listen to the music which inspired and motivations you, try playing it, it's okay to fail, acknowledge that these artists had years of practice. Watch and read about the cutting edge research which sound cool to you, attend talks... most of it will go over your head, but that's how it should be. The purpose of this type of activity is simply to through enough material to your mind that it can play with it and make connections.

Remember System 1 - System 2 thinking (that Nobel laureate thingy). The above is essentially a consequence of that only. Craft focused is for System 2, the slow, rational, logical, energy intensive mind. And Creativity focused is for System 1 - the fast, intuitive, creative mind.

Craft focused provides you with the push from below and creativity focused provides you the motivation, the pull from above. When you have both, the climb is much easier.

For example, if you want to learn quark confinement, directly jump to that topic in the books. Of course, most will go over your head but that's alright! The purpose is not to understand anyways! For now just collect words and ideas, whatever little you can gather. This is the creativity focused brain food for your system 1 mind. Then when you are doing craft focused activity of learning the basics from ground up, it will be much easier to find motivations as you can connect them to them terms and ideas that you have been collecting. It's same in, say, learning Guitar or Piano or Dance etc.

Notice, you might feel overwhelmed and feel like there is so much that you don't know etc. Again, these emotions are not helpful, meditate on the task at hand and let them flow. Then only, you will be able to collect large enough dataset of words and concepts that you would like to learn but don't know yet, in your mind.

# Acceptance Criteria of an Answer
We ask ourselves just so many questions but we commonly forget to do the most critical step that is to be very clear about the acceptance criteria of an answer. 2 illustrate but I mean say you ask me "what is 2 + 2?" and I answer "2 + 2". You can't say I am wrong but that's not the answers you expected! The subtle point here is, there was an implicit acceptance criteria for the answer which was you wanted the real number answer which when substituted on one side of the equation satisfies the relation "2+2=x".

When we ask questions like -
- what is life?
- what is the purpose of life? 
- what is god?
- does god exist? 
- what is good and bad?
- am I born for what I'm doing? 
- am I chasing the wrong goal?
- etc

We unfortunately, do not pay enough attention to the acceptance criteria, that is, given a candidate answer, how will you confirm or deny whether or not that's a good answer?


# Beware of 'Suitcase Words'
[Beware of the 'Suitcase Words'. - 2020 Vision Leader](https://2020visionleader.com/beware-the-suitcase-words/)

Beware of using "suitcase words"—terms that are packed with multiple meanings and assumptions. They can obscure clarity and hinder productive discussion. Like, "God", "meaning", "Good", "Bad", "purpose of life", "meditation" etc.

One way to deal with it is, following Eliezer Yudkowsky, try to say or ask the same thing but without use the 'suitcase word'. Increamently, remove suitcase words from your inquiry keeping the intended meaning the same!
